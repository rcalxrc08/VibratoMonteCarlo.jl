About this Package

VibratoMonteCarlo.jl is a Julia package containing some useful Financial functions for sensitivity computation.
VibratoMonteCarlo.jl is built on top of FinancialMonteCarlo.jl and FinancialFFT.jl. Standard montecarlo methods lacks of differentiability, which makes automatic differentiation useless. The main aim of this package is to provide a feasible way to compute sensitivities of any order for various types of payoffs using montecarlo methods.

The implementation for Vibrato Montecarlo is heavily based on the Master Thesis of Nicola Scaramuzzino (that is me) and the subsequent development.

Conditional Expectation

The payoff is not smooth, but its conditional expectation is. Let's fix τ<T\tau < Tτ<T, let's call h=T−τh = T - \tauh=T−τ and let's assume that the underlying process is a Levy process. Since:

V0=E(Φ(XT))=E(E(Φ(XT)∣Xτ))V_0=\mathbb{E}(\Phi(X_T))=\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}(\Phi(X_T)|X_{\tau}))V0​=E(Φ(XT​))=E(E(Φ(XT​)∣Xτ​)) ∂θnV0=∂θnE(E(Φ(XT)∣Xτ))=E(∂θnE(Φ(XT)∣Xτ))\partial^{n}_{\theta} V_0=\partial^{n}_{\theta} \mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}(\Phi(X_T)|X_{\tau}))=\mathbb{E}(\partial^{n}_{\theta} \mathbb{E}(\Phi(X_{T})|X_{\tau}))∂θn​V0​=∂θn​E(E(Φ(XT​)∣Xτ​))=E(∂θn​E(Φ(XT​)∣Xτ​))

The inner expectation is a smooth function of XτX_{\tau}Xτ​ and of the parameters, even if Φ\PhiΦ is a kink or a jump. Hence, when it is known in closed form, automatic differentiation can go through it path by path, at any order.

Conditional Saltando Expectation

Let's assume now that XtX_tXt​ is a finite activity Levy process, like Kou or Merton, with jump intensity λ\lambdaλ and κ=E(eY1−1)\kappa = \mathbb{E}(e^{Y_1} - 1)κ=E(eY1​−1), where YiY_iYi​ are the jump sizes. Instead of a fixed τ\tauτ, let's take the time of the last jump before TTT, with τ=0\tau = 0τ=0 if there are no jumps.

This τ\tauτ is not a stopping time, since knowing it needs a look at the future of the path, so we condition on all the jumps and on the path up to τ\tauτ. After τ\tauτ there are no more jumps and the process is a Brownian motion with a modified drift, which means a Black and Scholes model with dividend yield d+λκd + \lambda \kappad+λκ. Hence the inner expectation is known in closed form, even if the process has jumps:

With h=T−τh = T - \tauh=T−τ, the same derivatives become: ∂SCλκ,call=N(d1)e−h(d+κλ)\partial_{S} C_{\lambda \kappa,\text{call}} = N\left( d_1 \right) e^{ - h \left( d + \kappa \lambda \right)}∂S​Cλκ,call​=N(d1​)e−h(d+κλ)

It has the same limits of the fixed τ\tauτ case, it works only for European and binary options, and the same ones of Vibrato Saltando: λ\lambdaλ and TTT can't be differentiated, since the last jump time depends on them.

Vibrato Montecarlo Method

The pathwise method needs a smooth payoff, while the LRM has weights whose variance grows with the order of the sensitivity. Vibrato uses the best of each: it differentiates the path pathwise up to a time τ<T\tau < Tτ<T, and applies the LRM only to what is left.

Let's fix τ<T\tau < Tτ<T, let's call h=T−τh = T - \tauh=T−τ and let's assume that the underlying process is a Levy process. Since:

Vibrato Saltando Montecarlo

Vibrato needs the law of the last step, and for a Levy process that is only available numerically. For Variance Gamma it can even be singular on a short step. Vibrato Saltando ("jumping" vibrato) chooses what to condition on so that the inner law is exactly Gaussian, for any number of time steps.

Finite activity processes Let's assume that XtX_tXt​ is a finite activity Levy process, like Kou or Merton:

and let's call τ\tauτ the time of the last jump before TTT, with τ=0\tau = 0τ=0 if there are no jumps. This τ\tauτ is random and it is not a stopping time, since knowing it needs a look at the future of the path. So we condition on all the jumps and on WτW_{\tau}Wτ​: the jumps are independent of WWW, and after τ\tauτ there is nothing left but the diffusion. Numerical Refinements

The previous sections are exact in theory; the following refinements make the inner expectation accurate in practice. In all of them the inner law is Gaussian, XT∣Xτ∼N(μ,s2)X_T | X_{\tau} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, s^2)XT​∣Xτ​∼N(μ,s2), and the inner expectation is computed on the standardized variable Z=(XT−μ)/sZ = (X_T - \mu)/sZ=(XT​−μ)/s.

Payoff Centering For every θ\thetaθ the density integrates to one:

∫Rf(x,θ)dx=1\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(x,\theta) dx = 1∫R​f(x,θ)dx=1

hence all of its derivatives vanish and, for n≥1n \geq 1n≥1:

E(Bn({∂θilog⁡f(XT,θ)}i=1:n))=0\mathbb{E}(B_n(\{\partial^{i}_{\theta} \log{f(X_T,\theta)}\}_{i = 1 : n})) = 0E(Bn​({∂θi​logf(XT​,θ)}i=1:n​))=0

Therefore any constant ccc not depending on θ\thetaθ can be subtracted from the payoff without changing the sensitivities:

∂θnV0=E((Φ(XT)−c)Bn({∂θilog⁡f(XT,θ)}i=1:n))\partial^{n}_{\theta} V_0 = \mathbb{E}((\Phi(X_T) - c) B_n(\{\partial^{i}_{\theta} \log{f(X_T,\theta)}\}_{i = 1 : n}))∂θn​V0​=E((Φ(XT​)−c)Bn​({∂θi​logf(XT​,θ)}i=1:n​))

We take c=Φ(μ)c = \Phi(\mu)c=Φ(μ), the payoff at the centre of the inner density, and add it back to the price. The price is unchanged, while the score terms are now multiplied by Φ(μ+sZ)−Φ(μ)≈Φ′(μ)sZ\Phi(\mu + s Z) - \Phi(\mu) \approx \Phi'(\mu) s ZΦ(μ+sZ)−Φ(μ)≈Φ′(μ)sZ instead of Φ(μ+sZ)\Phi(\mu + s Z)Φ(μ+sZ). In vibrato s=σT−τs = \sigma \sqrt{T - \tau}s=σT−τ​ is small and the score terms grow like s−ns^{-n}s−n, so without centering the variance of the delta estimator grows like s−2s^{-2}s−2; with centering it stays bounded. Together with antithetic pairs ±Z\pm Z±Z, the second order term becomes 12(Φ(μ+sZ)+Φ(μ−sZ))−Φ(μ)≈12Φ′′(μ)s2Z2\frac{1}{2}(\Phi(\mu + s Z) + \Phi(\mu - s Z)) - \Phi(\mu) \approx \frac{1}{2}\Phi''(\mu) s^2 Z^221​(Φ(μ+sZ)+Φ(μ−sZ))−Φ(μ)≈21​Φ′′(μ)s2Z2, which compensates the s−2s^{-2}s−2 of the gamma score. In practice this reduces the noise of the gamma estimated with inner Monte Carlo by a factor 10 to 80.

Independent Inner Samples The inner expectation is estimated with MMM samples Z1,…,ZMZ_1, \dots, Z_MZ1​,…,ZM​ for each of the NNN outer paths. If the same samples were used for every outer path, the inner error would be the same for all paths and would not average out: the estimator would converge, as N→∞N \to \inftyN→∞, to a biased value with error of order M−1/2M^{-1/2}M−1/2 (deltas 8-15% off with M=200M = 200M=200). Drawing new inner samples for every outer path makes the inner errors independent, and the total error decreases with both NNN and MMM. With quasi Monte Carlo inner samples, the same Sobol points are randomly shifted modulo 1 for every outer path (Cranley-Patterson rotation), which keeps each inner estimate unbiased.
Quadrature Alternatively, the inner expectation can be computed as a deterministic integral on a uniform grid in ZZZ. The grid must reach far enough in the tails: for a call the payoff grows exponentially in the upper tail, and truncating at ±5\pm 5±5 standard deviations loses enough of the second order score terms to bias the volga by 3% to 45% on Variance Gamma. The default grid spans ±7\pm 7±7 standard deviations.

American Options

An American option, exercisable at the dates tk=k ht_k = k\,htk​=kh, k=1,…,Nk = 1, \dots, Nk=1,…,N, h=T/Nh = T/Nh=T/N, has the price

V0=sup⁡τE(e−rτΦ(Xτ))V_0 = \sup_{\tau} \mathbb{E}(e^{-r \tau} \Phi(X_{\tau}))V0​=τsup​E(e−rτΦ(Xτ​))

The exercise policy is estimated with the Longstaff-Schwartz algorithm: at each date the continuation value is regressed on 1,S,S21, S, S^21,S,S2 over the in the money paths, and the option is exercised where the payoff exceeds it. The regression uses values only, so the policy carries no derivative.

Why freezing the exercise is not enough The usual approach freezes the stopping time of each path and differentiates the cash flows as for a European contract. Since the policy is optimal, the first order effect of the moving exercise boundary vanishes and the first order sensitivities are correct. The second order ones are not: the boundary contributes a term proportional to the jump of the slope of the value at the boundary, and a frozen stopping time removes it. For a put, the frozen gamma only counts the paths that reach maturity near the strike, so it is even below the European gamma (-23% to -61% on the Longstaff-Schwartz put with K=40K = 40K=40).
Vibrato over every exercise date A policy frozen in price space, i.e. an exercise region EkE_kEk​ of prices at each date, does not have this problem for the sensitivities with respect to S0S_0S0​: the optimal exercise boundary does not depend on S0S_0S0​, so the policy is optimal for every S0S_0S0​, and the error of the regressed boundary enters every derivative with respect to S0S_0S0​ only at second order. The discontinuity of the exercise decision is then handled as the payoff at maturity: by vibrato. The paths are simulated at s0=0s_0 = 0s0​=0 and sk=(k−12)hs_k = (k - \frac{1}{2}) hsk​=(k−21​)h, so that every exercise date is the midpoint of [sk,sk+1][s_k, s_{k+1}][sk​,sk+1​] and, given the ends, the log price at tkt_ktk​ is a Brownian bridge:

Xtk∣Xsk,Xsk+1∼N(Xsk+Xsk+12,σ2h4)X_{t_k} | X_{s_k}, X_{s_{k+1}} \sim \mathcal{N}(\frac{X_{s_k} + X_{s_{k+1}}}{2}, \frac{\sigma^2 h}{4})Xtk​​∣Xsk​​,Xsk+1​​∼N(2Xsk​​+Xsk+1​​​,4σ2h​)

The bridge values are conditionally independent, and the future of a path depends on XtkX_{t_k}Xtk​​ only through the decision at tkt_ktk​. The value of each path is computed backwards:

WN=E(e−rTΦ(XT)∣XsN)W_N = \mathbb{E}(e^{-r T} \Phi(X_T) | X_{s_N})WN​=E(e−rTΦ(XT​)∣XsN​​) Wk=E(1Xtk∈Eke−rtkΦ(Xtk)+1Xtk∉EkWk+1∣Xsk,Xsk+1)W_k = \mathbb{E}(1_{X_{t_k} \in E_k} e^{-r t_k} \Phi(X_{t_k}) + 1_{X_{t_k} \notin E_k} W_{k+1} | X_{s_k}, X_{s_{k+1}})Wk​=E(1Xtk​​∈Ek​​e−rtk​Φ(Xtk​​)+1Xtk​​∈/​Ek​​Wk+1​∣Xsk​​,Xsk+1​​)

and V0=E(W1)V_0 = \mathbb{E}(W_1)V0​=E(W1​). Every conditional expectation is computed with the LRM over the bridge density, so the exercise indicator and the payoff are never differentiated: the estimator is smooth in all the parameters and unbiased for the chosen policy. The price of this exactness is variance: at the exercise boundary the integrand jumps by the gap between the exercise value and the realized continuation, and the weights of a bridge of standard deviation σh/2\sigma \sqrt{h} / 2σh​/2 amplify it, more and more as the number of exercise dates grows.

The bridge is Gaussian only for Black-Scholes dynamics. For Lévy and Heston models the exercise decision for tkt_ktk​ is taken at tk−1t_{k-1}tk−1​, the payoff is replaced by its vibrato expectation over the step, and the decisions are frozen: their first order sensitivities are accurate, the second order ones are biased.
